Before you begin

FoodRx generates general nutritional guidance using AI, based on publicly known dietary patterns for each condition. It is not a medical device and does not replace advice from your doctor or a registered dietitian.

Always confirm significant dietary changes with a qualified professional, especially if you take medication or manage multiple conditions.

      How this works

      FoodRx takes the conditions you select and sends them to a language model prompted to act as a clinical dietitian. It looks at where the dietary rules for each condition agree, and where they pull in different directions — a low-sugar food that's high in sodium, for instance — and resolves that overlap into a single, combined list.

      What it's good for

      A quick, plain-language starting point when you're standing in a kitchen or a grocery aisle trying to reconcile advice for more than one condition at once.

      What it isn't

      It isn't a diagnosis, a meal plan, or a substitute for a dietitian who knows your labs, medications and history. Portion sizes, drug-food interactions and condition severity aren't accounted for. Every list ends with the same reminder: confirm with your doctor before changing how you eat.

      Your data
